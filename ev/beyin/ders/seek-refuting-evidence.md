2026-11-03 the user said: while researching, look at the parts that refute an idea — then you know whether it is right and don't waste effort.

**Why:** supporting evidence is easy to find for almost any idea (survivorship, snapshot bias); one solid counter-case saves building on a wrong premise. Example that worked: the yt_ad "is paid" signal was only trusted after a control group of ≤300-sub channels showed 0/41 ads vs 31/35 for ≥1k subs.

**How to apply:** for every claim in an analysis, name the observation that would break it and check that first (control group, equal-age cohort, counter-search on the web, the flop cases). Report the verdict (holds / weakened / refuted) next to the claim. Related: [[channel-pick-consistency]], [[nis-product-findings]].
